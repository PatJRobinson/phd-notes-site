# Methodological Positioning

## Purpose

This page presents the empirical-method strand as a current methodological position. It explains why the study uses structured assumption elicitation together with reflexive, abductive thematic analysis, and why practitioner accounts are treated as a legitimate site of evidence rather than anecdotal background.

## Current position

The method is best described as contextualist, abductive, and reflexive. It treats assumptions as real in their effects: they shape whether robotic systems work, fail, remain explainable, or remain defensible. At the same time, those assumptions are accessed through partial artefacts, situated accounts, tool traces, standards, configuration, and interpretation. This means the study should not pretend that assumptions are directly measurable variables.

The most useful current formulation is a two-stage analytic architecture:

1. an assumption-elicitation protocol extracts comparable records from heterogeneous sources;
2. reflexive, theory-informed thematic analysis turns those records into explanatory themes.

This preserves structure without turning the method into a fixed codebook too early.

Two newer clarifications matter. First, assumptions should be used as a sensitising concept rather than a universal explanation for integration problems. Second, scenario should be treated as a provisional comparative term for how standards, engineering artefacts, and Rotifer experiments bound a system situation for explanation, execution, testing, comparison, or assurance.

## Why this matters

The thesis object is cross-layer assumptions in robotics systems. These assumptions are distributed across middleware, standards, tools, simulation, deployment context, organisational responsibility, and local practice. No single source type contains them cleanly.

The method therefore needs both comparability and interpretive depth. Comparability comes from asking similar extraction questions across artefacts and accounts: expected behaviour, required conditions, enforcement, evidence, breakdown, and responsibility. Interpretive depth comes from analysing how these records relate across layers and how practitioners make systems work despite incomplete representations.

## Source-grounded account

The internal methodology notes already define an assumption as a condition that must hold for a system, abstraction, or mechanism to function as intended, but which is not fully enforced or guaranteed by that system itself. The 2026-07-07 addenda refine this: the extraction framework should be called an assumption-elicitation protocol, not a fixed thematic codebook. The 2026-07-12 addenda refine it again: assumption dimensions are prompts for noticing differences across artefacts and cases, not a validated typology.

The scenario addendum gives the method another possible comparison object. It asks how standards, tools, experiment definitions, assurance contexts, and deployment descriptions bound the situation being analysed. This should remain open until source analysis shows whether scenario is one useful concept or several related concepts.

The new literature notes strengthen this position:

- [Braun and Clarke](../../../01_literature/hci-infrastructure-practice/braun-clarke-reflexive-thematic-analysis.md) supports reflexive thematic analysis as interpretive work, with theory used as sensitising concepts rather than as a predetermined coding frame.
- [Hollnagel](../../../01_literature/safety-resilience-theory/hollnagel-resilience-engineering-work-as-done.md) supports the claim that work-as-done is an empirical site, not merely an implementation detail.
- [Hutchins](../../../01_literature/hci-infrastructure-practice/hutchins-distributed-cognition.md) supports analysing understanding as distributed across people, artefacts, representations, environments, and time.
- [Star and Ruhleder](../../../01_literature/hci-infrastructure-practice/star-ruhleder-infrastructure.md) supports the idea that infrastructure and assumptions become visible through breakdown, installed base, invisible work, and standards.
- [Suchman](../../../01_literature/hci-infrastructure-practice/suchman-situated-action.md) supports the limit of formal representations: plans, policies, launch files, and architecture diagrams do not fully determine situated action.

## Relation to the RQs

- RQ1: the method asks how integrators construct working understanding through tools, traces, accounts, documentation, and local practice.
- RQ3: the method examines how safety/security-relevant concerns become visible, actionable, bypassed, or displaced across interfaces.
- RQ4: the method supports analysis of how evidence and assurance claims remain meaningful, drift, or become stale over time.
- RQ2: boundary and deployment concepts provide the cross-layer structure that interviews and artefact analysis can probe.

## What is currently evidence

The current evidence is methodological and design evidence. The notes establish a defensible study design, a coding posture, an interview guide, and a conceptual map from RQs to participant-facing topics. The literature notes provide public grounding for reflexive analysis, work-as-done, distributed cognition, situated action, and infrastructure.

There are not yet participant findings. Any compiled claim about practitioner behaviour should be phrased as a research expectation, analytic target, or candidate mechanism until empirical data exists.

## What is only design scaffold

The candidate domains in the interview guide are sensitising concepts, not findings. Responsibility boundaries, abstraction trust, operational pressure, visibility, lifecycle drift, assumption dimensions, scenario-like constructs, and cross-boundary coordination are current analytic lenses. They may become themes later, but they should not be reported as empirical themes until participant data has been analysed.

## What remains unresolved

The main risk is method drift. If the extraction protocol becomes too rigid, the study may no longer be reflexive thematic analysis in the Braun-and-Clarke sense. It may need to be renamed as framework analysis, codebook thematic analysis, or a hybrid structured qualitative analysis.

A second risk is concept drift. If assumption or scenario become umbrella labels for too many phenomena, the method loses analytical precision.
