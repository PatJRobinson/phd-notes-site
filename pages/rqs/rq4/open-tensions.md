# RQ4 Open Tensions

## Live tensions

- RQ4 is retained for now, but it must justify what change-over-time adds beyond RQ3's safety/security-across-interfaces question.
- RQ2 vs RQ4 boundary: recent feedback suggests the distinction may not be visible enough. RQ2 concerns composition, boundary-making, and integration conditions; RQ4 concerns assurance-claim validity and maintenance under change.
- Assurance vs safety/security: assurance is about claims/evidence/reasoning, not the property itself.
- Continuous assurance: useful term, but must not imply the project solves it.
- Scenario and assurance context: scenario-like constructs may help define the scope in which claims and evidence remain meaningful, but this needs standards analysis before it becomes a strong RQ4 move.
- Rotifer's role: evidence tracing supports assurance thinking but is not full assurance.
- Standards/literature anchoring remains thin in the organised notes.

## Historical tensions that may still matter

- MOSA and modularity material may become central to RQ4 if the thesis foregrounds change and recomposition.
- Human-as-overseer language may fit RQ4 if assurance maintenance is framed as interpretive work.

## Superseded tensions

- "Can adequate assurance be established under continuous change?" is too large as a direct thesis promise. The current RQ asks how change affects validity and maintenance.

## Decisions needed from me

- Should RQ4 be a full empirical/theoretical chapter, a synthesis chapter, or both?
- What exactly does RQ4 add that is not covered by RQ3? Current best answer: change over time, evidence validity, and maintenance of assurance claims.
- What exactly does RQ4 add that is not covered by RQ2? Current best answer: the status of claims and evidence after system composition, boundaries, or operating contexts change.
- How much assurance standards material needs to be integrated next?
- Should Rotifer evidence objects be explicitly framed as assurance artefacts or only as research evidence?

## Risks if unresolved

- RQ4 may overpromise continuous assurance.
- RQ4 may be read as duplicating RQ2 if the claim/evidence boundary is not made explicit.
- Assurance may collapse into safety/security.
- The thesis may lack enough concrete assurance examples.
