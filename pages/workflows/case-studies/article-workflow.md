# Article Development

## Research note versus public article

A case-study research note is exploratory. It gathers evidence, records uncertainty, tests analytical relevance, and preserves provenance. It can remain rough while the case is still being understood.

A finished public article is an authored output. It should have a clear argument, appropriate sources, defined audience, and stable prose. Mature articles should live under [06_outputs](../../../06_outputs/) rather than being treated as disposable generated text.

## Lifecycle

Use this progression path as a guide:

1. Candidate.
2. Evidence gathering.
3. Working case-study note.
4. Analytical review.
5. Article proposal.
6. First-class output under [06_outputs](../../../06_outputs/).
7. Presentation in the organised notes and public site.

## Evidence standards

Each case or article should identify:

- source notes and public sources used;
- which claims are supported by existing evidence;
- which claims are interpretations;
- which details are missing or uncertain;
- whether the case is being used as an example, a contrast, a source of evidence, or a public explanation;
- how it relates to one or more research questions.

## Boundaries

The organised notes may place, link, and present mature articles. They should not silently rewrite the substantive argument of an authored article. If a case is promising but under-evidenced, the page should record the evidence gap rather than making the case sound complete.
